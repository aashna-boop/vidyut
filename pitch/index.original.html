03 · How it works

  1. Sense

    Voltage, angles, load and sunlight — pulled from meters the utility already owns. Nothing new gets installed to start.

  2. Predict

    Two in-house models — a curtailment forecaster and a fault-theft localizer — are the core IP. Everything else wraps them.

  3. Deliver

    Switching commands and forecasts, wired into SCADA / DERMS as recommendations or direct commands.

04 · Product 1 — Curtailment prediction

One hour ahead, per topology: will part of this grid need to throttle solar? A temporal graph neural network and gradient-boosted trees, benchmarked against naive baselines.

0 ptsabove the naive persistence baseline (81–84% event F1)
0%of test hours curtailed in the Indian-solar variant — 2.4× the German data
Method

Trained chronologically — 2015–17 train, 2018 validation, 2019 test, never touched — and retrained per topology. A no-lag GBM scores within ~0.001: the skill is more than repeating the last value.

Boundary

Events are defined by the team's rule applied to real power-flow physics — proof the model learned that rule, not yet that it matches a utility's actual curtailment behavior.

05 · Product 2 — Fault & theft localization

When an unusual draw appears, Vidyut matches the live disturbance against a library of fingerprints — what a problem at each bus would look like — by closest match, and names the culprit.

0%top-grid accuracy at full voltage + angle + current sensing
0%located within 5 hops on the best grid, full sensing
0%still within 5 hops at only 30% sensor coverage
0trials per topology — 30 real operating states × 10 faults
Fixed

An operating-state mismatch bug was found and fixed along the way: it dropped exact-bus accuracy from 98% to ~41%. Fingerprints now match the actual pre-fault state — the PV and load level SCADA already knows.

Boundary

Voltage-only sensing is weak (6–65% exact). The full voltage + angle + current picture is where the method sings — all of it readable from existing instrumentation.

07 · Maintenance & grid systems

Two supporting layers ship with the same honesty standard: no invented accuracy numbers, no phantom utility connection.

a

Thermal wear ranking

A physics-based stress ranking — cumulative (loading%)² per line, an I²R heating proxy over every simulated day, shown line-by-line in the 3D viewer. No failure predictor is claimed, because no public dataset of real grid-equipment failures exists to validate one against. Invented extrapolations were removed.

b

SCADA / DERMS bridge (mock)

IEC 61850 object naming plus a DNP3-style point table; a working point database, state publishing from real power flow, and FISR switching through select-before-operate. After a run, its load-served and de-energized-bus state matches the physics engine digit for digit — 60.2525 MW vs 60.2525 MW. No wire protocol or utility system yet; none was available.

08 · Why Vidyut wins

The competitive field splits three ways — dashboards that measure, hardware that installs, and decks that promise. Vidyut sits in the empty quadrant.

a

Models, not dashboards

The core IP is two self-built ML models — protected by the hardest thing to copy: the dataset behind them. Analytics suites re-sell dashboards that measure the grid; Vidyut closes the loop and sends the switching command.

b

Runs on what's installed

No hardware pilot phase, no capex handshake: voltage, angles, load and sunlight come from meters and SCADA the utility already owns. A competitor that needs new sensors sells a board meeting; Vidyut sells a go-live.

c

Checked against physics

Every claim on this page came from AC power flow on published networks — restoration commands validated digit for digit against the physics engine, baselines included. Deck promises are cheap; physics is the referee.

Software-first. Sensor-light. Physics-checked.

09 · What's real, what isn't

Real

Published grid topologies (179-bus Oberrhein, 15-bus CIGRE MV, two Kerber feeders), Newton-Raphson AC power flow every simulated hour (~44k–50k hours per topology), five years of recorded OPSD/ENTSO-E weather and demand — 1,816 fully observed days — plus real NASA POWER solar for the Indian variant, 2,099 days.

Injected

Fault and theft events are simulated — no utility anywhere publishes real incident data. Curtailment labels come from the team's own rule, applied to real physics. And the grids themselves are German benchmarks, not an Indian feeder.

Verdict

A rigorous, honestly-caveated proof-of-concept on real physics and real weather data — not yet a live Indian utility grid.
